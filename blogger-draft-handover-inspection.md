# How to Prepare Your Car for a Detailing Visit and Inspect It at Handover

Most advice about detailing is about choosing a studio. Less is said about the two moments that decide whether you are happy afterwards: the day you drop the car off, and the ten minutes when you collect it. Both are easy to rush, and both are cheap to do properly.

This guide gives you a short routine for each. It works at any studio.

## Before you drop the car off

### 1. Remove what you do not want touched
Take out valuables, documents, loose change, charging cables, child seats and anything on the floor or in door pockets. Staff can then clean every surface without moving your things, and nothing goes missing by mistake. Leave the boot clear too.

### 2. Photograph the car as it is
Take clear photos in daylight of every side, the roof, the wheels, the bumpers and the interior. Include any existing scratches, dents, stone chips or stains, and a photo of the odometer and fuel level. If you and the studio ever disagree about what was there before, the photos settle it quickly. A reputable studio will usually do its own check with you at drop-off; the photos are your copy.

### 3. Point out what worries you
Tell the studio about anything you want looked at: a swirl-marked bonnet, water spots on the glass, a stained seat, a smell. Also mention anything you do not want done, for example if you want the engine bay left alone. It is much easier to agree this before work starts.

### 4. Ask what is included and what is not
Ask for the services in writing: which areas, which steps, and whether anything that looks included (door shuts, wheel arches, boot area) is extra. Ask when the car will be ready and whether the date depends on anything, such as weather or product curing. `[P91: add how your studio confirms scope and timing, if there is a standard process.]`

### 5. Ask about aftercare before you leave
Some treatments come with instructions about washing, parking or leaving the car alone for a while. Ask what applies to your job and ask for it in writing, so you are not relying on memory when you collect.

## At handover: a ten-minute inspection

Do this at the studio, not at home. If something is wrong, it is far easier to fix before you drive away.

### Use good light
Ask to see the car outside in daylight, or under strong, bright light. Studio lighting is designed to make paint look good. Sunlight, or a torch held at a low angle, shows what the work actually achieved.

### Check the paint
- **Walk around slowly** and look along each panel from a low angle, not straight on.
- **Look for swirl marks and fine scratches.** They show up as circles or arcs in direct light. Compare against your photos: the goal is that things are better than before, or at least no worse.
- **Check for polish or product residue** in panel gaps, around badges and emblems, at the edges of lights and in door handles.
- **Look at black plastic and rubber trim.** White or chalky marks along trim edges can be dried polishing compound.
- **Run a hand over the paint.** It should feel smooth and clean, without a gritty or sticky patch.

### Check the glass and lights
Look at the windscreen and windows from inside and outside, in the angle where streaks show most. Check headlights and tail lights for residue or fresh haze.

### Check the wheels and tyres
Wheel faces and spokes should be clean, including the inner barrel where you can see it. Tyre shine should be even. If it has been applied heavily, it can sling onto the paint, so ask how it should be wiped off if so.

### Check the interior
- Sit in the car. Does it smell clean, without an overpowering fragrance or a damp smell?
- Press carpets and seat fabric. If they feel wet, ask how long they need to dry and how they should be aired.
- Check vents, cup holders, door pockets, the dashboard seams and the gaps between seats and console.
- Make sure the screen, buttons, windows and seat controls all work as before.
- Look at the boot and under the mats.

### Compare against your photos
Go back to your drop-off photos. Is every mark that was there before still there, and has nothing new appeared?

### Ask before you pay
If anything looks wrong, say so right away and calmly, and point to the exact spot. Most studios would rather fix a missed area while you are there. Ask them to explain what they did, since a good studio can describe each step.

## After you take the car home

- Follow the aftercare instructions you were given, especially in the first days.
- Wash by hand with a clean mitt and proper car shampoo, rather than a stiff brush wash.
- Dry with a clean microfibre towel.
- Check the car again after a few days. Some small issues, such as a missed patch or residue that seeped out of a gap, only show after a wash or a drive. A good studio will want to hear about it.

## Questions people ask

**Do I need to wash the car before taking it to a detailer?**
Usually not. The studio will clean it as part of the work. A quick rinse is not needed unless the studio asks for it.

**Should I be there while the work is done?**
Not usually, but ask. Some studios welcome you at certain stages and some prefer to work undisturbed. `[P91: state your own policy, if you have one.]`

**What if I find a problem after I get home?**
Take photos, note the time, and contact the studio the same day if you can. The sooner it is raised, the easier it is to put right.

**Is it normal for the car to look slightly different in the sun and in the shade?**
Yes. Paint, glass and coatings all look different in different light. That is why checking in daylight matters.
